## Ford (F) Dividend at a Glance

Ford Motor Company (NYSE: F) currently pays a **regular quarterly dividend of $0.15 per share**, which annualizes to **$0.60 per share** and implies a trailing-twelve-month yield of roughly **4.1%–4.3%** as of August 2026. The most recent declaration carries an ex-dividend date of August 11, 2026, with payment scheduled for September 1, 2026.

That yield sits in the middle of the auto sector: far above General Motors (GM) at under 1%, but well below high-payout European names like Volkswagen (8%+) and Mercedes-Benz (7%+).

## How Ford's Dividend Is Structured

Ford uses a **two-layer dividend model**: a steady regular quarterly payment plus an occasional **supplemental dividend** when earnings and cash flow are strong. This is visible in the 2025 schedule, where the February payment was $0.30 (a $0.15 regular plus a $0.15 supplemental), while the other three quarters were $0.15 each — totaling $0.75 for the year (source: Ford Investor Relations; Morningstar).

The supplemental is **not a commitment**. It appears after strong years (2023 paid up to $0.80 in February; 2024 paid $0.33) and disappears when cash is needed elsewhere — most notably during the electric-vehicle transition.

## The Recovery From the 2020 Cut

Ford did not eliminate its dividend in 2020, but it slashed the annual payout from $0.60 (2019) to $0.15, then $0.10 in 2021, before rebuilding through 2022–2023 (source: Morningstar; MacroTrends).

The 5-year dividend-per-share growth rate of **+31.95%** (source: Investing.com) reflects a recovery off a very low 2021 base rather than a steadily rising payout. The 2023 trailing yield of 10%+ was driven more by a depressed ~$12 share price than by an unusually large dividend.

## Why the Payout Ratio Is Misleading for Ford

The payout ratio — dividends divided by earnings — is the usual safety gauge, but for Ford it swings wildly and sometimes turns meaningless.

| Year | Payout Ratio |
|---|---|
| 2022 | ~2.3% |
| 2023 | ~115.7% |
| 2024 | ~53.4% |
| 2020 | anomalous (negative EPS) |

When Ford posted a net loss (as in parts of 2020 and in some 2025 estimates where EPS is forecast near -$2.06), the ratio breaks down because the denominator is negative (source: MarketScreener). For a cyclical automaker, **free cash flow coverage** matters more than the accounting payout ratio.

> Key point: a 4% yield backed by $0.15 quarterly cash is sustainable only if Ford's adjusted free cash flow stays in the $3.5B–$4.5B range the company has guided for (source: Ford Q4 2024 earnings release, SEC filing).

## Is Ford's Dividend Safe?

The regular $0.15 quarterly dividend is supported by Ford's scale and liquidity — over $28B in cash and nearly $47B in total liquidity at end-2024 (source: Ford Q4 2024 earnings, SEC). The bigger question is the **supplemental**, which depends on EV losses, tariff costs (management cited roughly $2B in net tariff headwinds), and the pace of the Ford+ turnaround.

For income investors, the practical takeaway: treat the $0.60 annualized rate as the durable floor, and view any supplemental as a bonus. If you want steadier dividend growth, our [best dividend stocks 2025](/articles/best-dividend-stocks-2025/) guide covers names with longer raise streaks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Ford's current dividend?
Ford pays a regular **$0.15 per share quarterly**, annualizing to **$0.60 per share**, with a TTM yield around 4.1%–4.3% as of August 2026.

### How often does Ford pay dividends?
Quarterly. The regular schedule pays in March, June, September, and December, with the ex-dividend date roughly two months before payment.

### Did Ford cut its dividend?
Yes — Ford reduced its annual dividend to $0.15 in 2020 (from $0.60 in 2019) and to $0.10 in 2021, then rebuilt it through 2022–2023. It has not eliminated the dividend entirely.

### What is Ford's dividend yield compared with GM?
Ford's yield (~4.3%) is much higher than General Motors (~1.0%) as of 2026, though both are below high-payout European automakers.

### Is Ford's dividend safe?
The regular $0.15 quarterly payment is backed by strong liquidity, but the payout ratio is volatile because earnings swing with the auto cycle and EV losses. Free cash flow, not the ratio, is the better safety gauge.

### Does Ford pay a supplemental dividend?
Sometimes. Ford has paid supplemental dividends after strong years (e.g., $0.15 supplemental in February 2025). They are discretionary and not guaranteed.

### Where can I verify Ford's dividend dates?
Ford's own [Investor Relations](https://shareholder.ford.com/Information/stock/default.aspx) page and [NASDAQ's dividend history](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/F/dividend-history) list official ex-dividend and payment dates.
