## SCHD dividend date: the three dates that matter

| Date type | What it means | What an investor should do |
|---|---|---|
| Ex-dividend date | The first trading day when a new buyer generally does not receive the declared distribution | Own SCHD before this date if you want that distribution |
| Record date | The date the fund checks its shareholder records | Usually follows the ex-date under the current settlement framework |
| Payable date | The day the cash is scheduled to reach eligible shareholders' accounts | Check your broker if cash is not visible immediately |

Investor.gov explains that buying on or after the ex-dividend date generally means the buyer will not receive that dividend. The [SEC investor bulletin on ex-dividend dates](https://www.sec.gov/resources-for-investors/investor-alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/ex-dividend-dates-when-are-you-entitled-stock-cash-dividend) is the best place to review the settlement details.

### Why the September amount may not look like the others

SCHD's distribution is not a fixed coupon. The amount reflects income received by the fund and the index portfolio's underlying dividends. A single quarter can be higher or lower than the previous quarter, so comparing only one SCHD dividend date or one payment is a poor way to judge the ETF.

## What happens if you buy SCHD around the ex-date?

Imagine an investor buys 100 shares for $8,000 before the June 24, 2026 ex-date. At $0.2525 per share, the declared cash distribution would be $25.25 before tax.

| Holding | Distribution | Gross cash |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 shares | 100 × $0.2525 | $25.25 |
| 500 shares | 500 × $0.2525 | $126.25 |
| 1,000 shares | 1,000 × $0.2525 | $252.50 |

This is an illustrative calculation, not a reader testimonial. The distribution is not free money: around the ex-date, the fund's market price can adjust for the distribution, and the price can move for many other reasons. Taxes and reinvestment choices also change the result.

## How to find the next SCHD dividend date

1. Open Schwab's official SCHD page and select the distribution history.
2. Look for the newest declared ex-date, record date, payable date, and total distribution.
3. Check the number of shares you held before the ex-date.
4. Multiply shares by the per-share distribution to estimate gross cash.
5. Reconcile the final amount with your broker's 1099-DIV and account activity.

## Questions investors ask about SCHD dividend dates

### What is the next SCHD dividend date?

The next SCHD ex-dividend date is published by Schwab after the distribution is declared. Do not treat an estimated quarterly month as an official date.

### Do I need to hold SCHD on the payable date?

Usually no. Eligibility is tied to the ex-dividend and record-date rules, not to buying on the day cash arrives. Confirm unusual transactions with your broker.

### Can I buy SCHD on the ex-dividend date and still get paid?

Generally no. A purchase on the ex-dividend date normally does not qualify for that distribution, according to Investor.gov and the SEC.

### Does SCHD pay monthly or quarterly?

SCHD pays quarterly. The exact dates and amounts are set by Schwab and can vary.

### Is the SCHD dividend guaranteed?

No. ETF distributions can rise, fall, or change. The historical distribution table describes the past; it is not a promise.

### Where can I see the official SCHD dividend history?

Use Schwab Asset Management's SCHD page first. Third-party calendars can be useful for alerts, but the fund sponsor is the primary source for declared dates.
